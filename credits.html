Where the data, maps, flags, portraits and code on this site come from, and the terms they are used under.

Everything World Literacy teaches is built from public or openly licensed sources. Country data

Country facts and descriptions are from The World Factbook, 2024 edition, a publication of the United States Government in the public domain, served by the World Factbook Archive (worldfactbookarchive.org). The Factbook is cited as its publisher requests; this site is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Central Intelligence Agency, and the CIA seal is not used. The Factbook was discontinued in February 2026; the 2024 edition remains in the public domain.

Statistics

Country populations, the population rankings and the Global Trends series are from the World Bank, World Development Indicators, licensed CC BY 4.0. Indicators used: SP.POP.TOTL, SI.POV.DDAY, SH.DYN.MORT, SP.DYN.LE00.IN, SP.DYN.TFRT.IN, EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS, SH.H2O.BASW.ZS, IT.NET.USER.ZS, SH.IMM.MEAS, SN.ITK.DEFC.ZS, SP.URB.TOTL.IN.ZS and EN.GHG.ALL.MT.CE.AR5. Values are rounded and reformatted; the World Bank did not review this use.

Dates

The dates of the History events and the lifespans of the People were cross-checked against Wikidata (CC0, no rights reserved). Person pages link to the corresponding Wikipedia article for further reading; no Wikipedia text is reproduced.

Maps and software

Country geometry for the globe comes from Natural Earth (public domain), as redistributed in TopoJSON form by world-atlas (ISC licence) and rendered with D3.js v7.9.0 and topojson-client v3.1.0 (both ISC licence). The flat world map is world-states.svg from SVG-World-Map by Raphael Lepuschitz (MIT licence), whose country outlines derive from the Wikimedia Commons files BlankMap-World.svg (public domain) and Blank Map World Secondary Political Divisions.svg (CC0). The locator maps in the decks were drawn from the same Natural Earth data. The notices those licences require:

Figures cited from

The Geopolitics, Economics and Threads cards quote figures from the sources named on each card, among them the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI, sipri.org), the Federation of American Scientists, Ember (CC BY 4.0), the U.S. Energy Information Administration, UNCTAD, the International Energy Agency (CC BY 4.0), the International Monetary Fund, the World Bank, the World Trade Organization, TrendForce and national statistical offices.
